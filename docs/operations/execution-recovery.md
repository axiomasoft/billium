# Reconcile and recover payment execution

## Unknown attempt

1. Identify the original installation, seller, environment, intent owner and source authority from authenticated context. Retrieve the command receipt by key and original business identity with `ExecutionHistoryQueries`. Retrieve the attempt and current accepted material. Treat an unauthorized or unknown result as a stop for new effects until the original scope and identity are established.
2. Check whether the original attempt committed dispatch start. An unsent admit can remain held or be cancelled through the normal command if still cancellable. After dispatch starts, local cancel no longer settles what happened at the provider. Preserve the original evidence, credentials and reservation and schedule lookup by the admitted attempt. Use the persisted provider-assigned ID when available and qualified. Keep the exact original account, reference, action, currency and request window.
3. Submit signed responses and callbacks through custody and evidence processing. The processor may accept an immutable fact after the sender's lease ended. Check the reducer's accepted revision, qualified actual amount, and cumulative observed, applied and exception amounts. A stale worker's state update grants no authority. A matching replay can acknowledge retained custody or application without a second economic transition.
4. Keep capacity held while the outcome is ambiguous. A signed qualified exact zero resolution or an independently verified absence proof for the complete original send window may release capacity. Require proof of retired egress and blocked late sends where absence is used. Expired TTL, timeout, missing inventory page and an empty unqualified lookup keep the result unknown. Review a late external success as an actual effect and record any exposure beyond capacity separately.

## Reconciliation and protected repair

The targeted path uses `ReconciliationCommands::targeted()` on the exact original attempt. Pass the same scoped run reference on replay, a worker identity and lease. Admitted unsent work remains held. The qualified inventory path uses `ReconciliationCommands::inventory()` for the explicit source authority, run reference, merchant revision, currency and `[from, through]` window. It requires the authenticated `execution.reconcile` permission in addition to the selected owner and source grants. Resume with identical arguments. Each call advances at most one qualified page. Use a successor window with positive overlap and no forward gap. A `QualifiedInventoryDriver` binds the cursor, report identity, provider source digest, account revision and credential version. Treat verified continuation, terminal coverage, incomplete runs and typed unsupported driver or workflow as distinct conditions. A usable terminal page attests coverage through the requested endpoint. A completed report may contain unresolved review cases.

Compare observed facts with the retained original basis. Keep discrepancies and expected/observed values with their evidence and provenance. Settlement, fee, chargeback, dispute and provider manual operations have separate identities. A provider settlement net amount has no role as the captured or refundable amount. Hand off external-only or conflicting effects for qualified review and use the original custody/reducer path for resulting payment revisions.

`RepairCommands::repair()` takes the typed action and retained review reference. Use the independently authenticated operator with the original seller, installation, owner and source authority plus the `execution.repair` permission. The action's authorization reference must match the authenticated actor. Preserve the original `actionRef` and canonical digest for replay. A changed reviewed basis or action body requires a separately authorized action. Repair records a linked audited disposition or verified resolution and publishes the registered `execution.repair_recorded.v1` event in `billium.event.v3`. A manual request alone gives no permission to set provider totals, clear money holds, create a charge, post a refund, or overwrite an earlier event. Verify the persisted repair receipt and linked evidence before moving review state. A registered `payment.certified_no_effect.v1` event in `billium.event.v4` reports a separately verified no-effect terminal decision for the original request. It carries the certificate reference/digest and covered window, without claiming provider aggregate totals. Legacy feed v1/v2 selection requires separate negotiation for v3/v4 direct public events.

For a late refund after a fenced release and paid replacement, an operator may adjudicate the current inventory comparison using `resolve_discrepancy`. Supply the late observation and every independently applied replacement fact. Verification links the original release, exact discrepancy and unchanged root/contributing revisions. The resulting resolved review records the case disposition. The observed amount, last consistent applied amount, exposure and financial hold remain unchanged; a separate authorized monetary correction is required to repair the economic inconsistency.

`approve_linked_correction` audits delayed local application of a refund already present in the original provider gross-refund statement. Its immutable refund intent must name that stable statement reference in `reasonCode` and use `category=reconciliation_correction` before dispatch. Retained custody and the actual provider effect must precede the comparison. Approval checks the exact applied child, reservation, original statement delta and root/contributing revisions; unrelated same-amount captures or refunds do not qualify. It sends no provider request, leaves the review open for a subsequent complete consistent comparison, and does not infer a desired refund from a reported amount.

The repair digest is `sha256:` plus the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON object. Use the fixed `schema` value `billium.execution.repair.v1`, the `scope` object from `ExecutionScope`, `source` from the original execution profile, `root` from the original root ref, `review` from the latest immutable review event, `action` from the action kind, `actor` from authenticated caller context, and the supplied `reason` and ordered `evidence` refs. Place the review's matching original evidence first. Keep later refs in their original order on retry. Verification derives the economic comparison from retained data.

## Restore and handover

Before a planned handover, close old owner admission at the independent issuer, drain committed financial roots, and record the actual seal and egress retirement proof. Preserve the authority, provider, archival and receiver journals outside the database being restored. Retain the restored backup identity and the independently verified post-backup inventory checkpoint. A crash or unexplained continuity loss requires a recovery owner even when copied database counters match.

1. Keep restored copies quarantined at the external authority and egress boundary. Give the new attachment an independently issued recovery generation and scoped recovery process token. Preserve existing read and raw custody ingress. Keep outgoing provider effects and new collection work closed.
2. Rehearse backup and restore on an isolated database with the immutable original migration records and distinct owner/runtime roles. Record database version, commands and exit codes, backup identity/hash, schema/version/sequence and the counts before backup, after original progress, after restore, and after recovery. Verify restored runtime permissions and readiness. Use `Installation::openSession()` to open the new local epoch in recovery. Exercise restored sequences and raw evidence/work custody before admitting ordinary work. Qualify production PITR separately using the production backup and recovery procedures.
3. Reconcile the restored state against independently retained provider effects and downstream acknowledgements, including effects after the backup. Use original operation, attempt, provider identity and scoped observation partitions. Verify that replay preserves one external effect and one downstream financial application. Disposition any unresolved or exceptional exposure before qualifying the affected scope for activation. Keep a blocked or incomplete scope in recovery.
   Recover an effect whose local operation disappeared through `OriginalIdentityRecovery::recover()`, with the original account and inventory reference. The host-bound `OriginalIdentityArchive` authenticates the dispatch manifest and source journal outside the database and exposes only that exact cached object during import. The recovery owner imports the original business identity, binding and key; no dispatch work is created. Lookup uses the authenticated original reference or assigned provider ID, then the normal custody/reducer path. Missing or conflicting archives keep the namespace held.

4. Have the external authority adopt the immutable transition and issue the verified activation certificate for the exact new owner and coverage, including retirement proof. `Installation::activateSession()` adopts certified activation. Retain the transition reference for safe replay if the authority adopts before local commit. After the local commit, verify matching process evidence before lifting quarantine for the admitted scope. Keep the original owner and attachment fenced.
5. Roll forward with the same guarded recovery on migration or activation failure. Keep backup, authority receipt, original evidence and old code compatible with permitted historical reads. Treat a copied active flag, equal commit numbers or clean shutdown as diagnostic data only.

Use [payment execution operations](execution.md) for deployment inputs and read/query surfaces and [module lifecycle](module-lifecycle.md) for drain and retirement. Keep fiscal, product and accounting handoffs independently replayable across recovery.
